# Do the lamellar elastic media carry particles?

Generated by `elastic_matter_particles.js` on a 24x24 torus, deterministic
mulberry32 PRNG, no external packages. Every measurement is background-relative: the
reference at generation t is `bg[t mod 2]` and the object of interest is
`D(t) = config(t) XOR reference(t)`.

## Prediction, recorded before the run (control C4)

> "a defect in a lamellar phase should be able to travel ALONG a slab, so I expect at least
> one mover; I expect none to be able to cross the empty channels."

This was stated by the task giver before the script ran; the conclusion scores it.

## Definition used

- A **mover** is a group of deviation cells (at most 24 cells, **not** required to be a
  single 8-connected component: several interacting pieces are allowed, clustered when their
  centroids are within 2 cells) whose translation-canonical shape recurs with period
  `p <= 16` and whose centroid drifts by a **constant non-zero** integer vector every
  `p` generations, for at least 4 consecutive periods.
- The candidate must be the **only** group with that shape in its frame, so identity is unambiguous.
- **Velocity is measured relative to the material.** Some lamellar phases translate between the two
  frames of their cycle, so a defect that is merely *bound* to the material also changes its global
  position every frame. The per-frame material shift is measured from the reference cycle itself and
  subtracted, so a bound defect has zero material-relative velocity and is not called a particle.
- Perturbations are random connected blobs of k cells, added only in the vacuum or removed only
  from the matter; k = 1,2,3,5.

## Conclusion

The five lamellar media were perturbed with 950 independent defect injections
(k = 1, 2, 3, 5, additions into the channels and removals from the slabs) and evolved for up to
300 generations each. On the lamellar phase that carries the reported elasticity,
**5 of 5 rules produced ZERO movers** (0 distinct movers in total).

On the second medium tested for each rule (a different lamellar phase of the same rule, analysed
with the identical detector and the identical perturbation set), 573 further runs produced 4 distinct movers.

### Prediction check (C4)

The prediction was: *"a defect in a lamellar phase should be able to travel ALONG a slab, so I expect
at least one mover; I expect none to be able to cross the empty channels."*

- **No mover was found in any of the five elastic lamellar media.** The half of the prediction that
  expected at least one mover is therefore **not** supported on these media at this budget.
- **No excitation crossed the channels** either: the only movers that exist anywhere in this study are
  in a different, non-elastic lamellar phase (see limitations), and all of them have zero velocity
  across the stripes, i.e. they travel along the slabs and never cross a channel. That half of the
  prediction is supported.

### What this means

An elastic medium with no excitations is a wall with no ball: the five two-sidedly elastic lamellar
rules repair single-cell defects (E_add_causal = E_remove_causal = 1.0, verified again here) but
**nothing travels on them**. The defects do not merely fail to cross the channels; they do not travel
at all: they sit in place and are absorbed.

## Honest limitations

- The mover definition requires an exact recurring canonical shape with constant material-relative drift
  for at least 4 periods. A radiating, growing or chaotically deforming excitation is
  scored as no mover rather than as a weak one. The recurrence diagnostic in the log shows how often
  small groups recur at all and what displacements they show, so this restriction can be audited.
- Only a 24x24 torus and up to 300 generations; a mover that needs a longer run or
  a bigger lattice is missed.
- k is limited to 1,2,3,5 with a bounded number of positions; a mover that needs a
  specific larger or specially shaped defect is missed. Deviations larger than 24 cells are
  never treated as a mover, by construction.
- Several lamellar phases exist per rule and their elasticity differs. The phase used for the headline
  result is the one matching the reported density/area fingerprint; other phases are reported too, and
  one of them (density 0.25) is not elastic at all yet does carry tiny movers. A "no movers" answer is
  therefore stated for the elastic lamellar phases, not for every conceivable phase of these rules.
- "No mover" is a statement about this detector and this budget, not a proof of impossibility.
